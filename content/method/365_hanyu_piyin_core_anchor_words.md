In linguistics and lexicography, a sense is simply a distinct meaning or usage of a word. When CC-CEDICT separates meanings using slashes (/), it is separating them into distinct senses (e.g., Sense 1: spatial height vs. Sense 2: food type).
The only distinction to keep in mind:

To a linguist: A "sense" is the abstract semantic distinction itself.

To CC-CEDICT: A "sense" is the specific string between two / slashes containing one or more semicolon-separated glosses (synonyms).

Calling them senses aligns with both general linguistics and CC-CEDICT's official documentation.

### Example from the Core 365

```text
gāo 

Definition
 │
 ├── Sense 1: /cake/
 │     └── Gloss 1: "cake"
 │
 ├── Sense 2: /tall; high/
 │     ├── Gloss 1: "tall"
 │     └── Gloss 2: "high"
 │
 └── Sense 3: /paste; cream/
       ├── Gloss 1: "paste"
       └── Gloss 2: "cream"
```

By stepping away from traditional dictionary structures and moving toward handwritten semantic maps and tailored interactive drills, these 365 anchor words offer a practical framework for memorising modern Mandarin vocabulary.

When reading modern Mandarin written solely in Hanyu Pinyin, or listening to spoken Mandarin, the objective when encountering an unknown word is not to decode a character's dictionary entry in all its historical depth. The goal is to form a viable hypothesis quickly to stay in the flow.

* **Conventional Flashcards Ask:** *"Can I remember the dictionary definition?"*
* **Predictive Pinyin Asks:** *"If I encounter an unknown word with this pronunciation, what semantic neighborhood should I search in my mind first?"*

To achieve this, the 365 core anchor words rely on key design principles:

* **Task-Oriented Lossy Compression:** Discards low-utility dictionary baggage to maximize cognitive speed and real-time audio recognition.
* **Islands of Meaning:** Slashes (`/`) mark distinct semantic families; semicolons (`;`) capture near-synonyms or closely related variations within that cluster.
* **Prediction Power Over Frequency:** A semantic family earns a slot only if it unlocks multiple high-frequency compounds or eliminates impossible interpretations. If its productivity is limited to a single obscure word, it stays out.
* **Ordered by Vividness:** Entries focus on distinct mental images rather than flat synonyms when English translations overlap.
* **Verb Standardization:** All verb-based entries consistently begin with `"to "` to maintain immediate structural clarity.

### Core Foundations

* **Task-Oriented Lossy Compression:** Look at the most common senses. Deliberately discard rare meanings.
* **Islands of Meaning:** Slashes (`/`) mark distinct semantic families or "islands" in meaning space. Semicolons (`;`) capture near-synonyms or closely related variations within that same cluster.
* **Prediction Power Over Frequency:** A semantic family earns a slot only if it unlocks multiple high-frequency compounds or eliminates impossible interpretations during reading. If a productivity is limited to a single obscure word, it stays out.
* **Ordered by Vividness:** Families are ordered by memorability and cognitive utility rather than strict dictionary hierarchy. When English translations collide, the focus shifts to distinct mental images rather than flat synonyms.
* **Verbs Preceded by "to":** All verb-based entries consistently begin with "to " (e.g., `/to cook; to boil/`) to maintain structural clarity.

### Structural Rules

**1. Formatting Syntax**
* Distinct, unmergeable semantic domains are separated by slashes (`/`).
* Near-synonyms within a domain use semicolons (`;`).
* All active verbs are explicitly prefixed with "to " (e.g., `/to cook; to boil/`).

**2. Vividness & Atmosphere**
* Ordering inside the app quiz and on paper diagrams is dictated by memorability, transparent etymological roots, and distinct visual anchors—not strict dictionary priority.
* When two Pinyin cards share similar English terms, optimize for contrasting mental images over abstract synonyms.

**3. Ruthless Pruning**
* Rare pronunciations, literary oddities, standalone surnames, and non-productive character senses are treated as temporary guests, kept only if they contribute essential predictive value.

### Prediction Over Definition

When encountering spoken Mandarin or reading text formatted purely in Pinyin, decoding every character's historical depth slows down comprehension. The goal in real-time communication is simply to form a valid hypothesis and maintain rhythm.

* Traditional Flashcard Question: "Can I recall the full dictionary definition?"
* Predictive Pinyin Question: "If I hear or read this sound, which semantic island should my mind navigate to first?"

To achieve this, the 365 core anchor words rely on several key design principles:

* Task-Oriented Lossy Compression: Deliberately removes low-utility lexical weight to maximize cognitive speed and listening stamina.
* Islands of Meaning: Slashes (`/`) delineate distinct conceptual islands; semicolons (`;`) group closely related expressions within an island.
* Prediction Power Over Frequency: Slots are granted based on compound productivity and the ability to rule out impossible meanings in real time.
* Ordered by Vividness: Visual contrasts and distinct imagery take precedence over flat, overlapping translations.
* Verb Standardization: Active verbs consistently begin with "to " to maintain structural clarity across both handwritten maps and interactive app prompts.
